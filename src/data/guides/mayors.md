> **Who this is for:** Anyone opening or renewing a business in a Philippine city or municipality: sole proprietors (DTI), corporations and partnerships (SEC), and cooperatives (CDA).
>
> **What it costs:** It varies by LGU. Your bill includes local business tax, which is usually based on capital for new businesses and gross sales for renewals, plus regulatory fees set by your city's revenue code. Quezon City, for example, taxes new businesses on declared capitalization ([QC BPLD Citizen's Charter 2026](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/BPLD_2026-CC.pdf)). The fire safety inspection fee is 15% of the fees the LGU charges, with a minimum of ₱500 ([BFP MC 2021-020](https://bfp.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/MC-2021-020-SUPPLEMENTAL-GUIDELINES-ASSESSMENT-COLLECTION-OF-FIRE-CODE-FEES-REL.-TO-THE-IMPLEMENTATION-OF-RA-9514-OTHERWISE-KNOWN-AS-THE-FIRE-CODE-OF-THE-PHILIPPINES-OF-2008-ITS-REVISED-IRR-OF-2019.pdf)).
>
> **How long it takes:** By law, no more than 3 working days for simple, 7 for complex, and 20 for highly technical or high-risk applications ([RA 11032 Sec. 9](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11032_2018.html)). Quezon City estimates 1 to 3 days ([QC guide](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qcitizen-guides/opening-a-business-in-qc/)).

## How the process works now
Under the Ease of Doing Business Act, you no longer go office by office. Every city and municipality must ([RA 11032 Sec. 11](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11032_2018.html)):
- Use **one unified application form**. It covers local taxes, building, sanitary, zoning and other clearances, including the BFP fire clearance.
- Process it at a **Business One-Stop Shop (BOSS)**, in person or online (e-BOSS).
- Handle **barangay clearance** at the city or municipal level.
- Issue **sanitary and other local clearances together with the permit**.

## Before you start
- [ ] Register your business name with DTI, or your company with SEC or CDA ([QC guide](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qcitizen-guides/opening-a-business-in-qc/)).
- [ ] Prepare proof of your business location: a tax declaration or title if you own it, or a notarized lease if you rent ([QC guide](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qcitizen-guides/opening-a-business-in-qc/)).
- [ ] Take photos of the location, or draw a sketch, as your LGU requires ([QC guide](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qcitizen-guides/opening-a-business-in-qc/)).
- [ ] Have the owner's valid ID ready ([QC guide](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qcitizen-guides/opening-a-business-in-qc/)).
- [ ] If your building already has an occupancy-stage Fire Safety Inspection Certificate (FSIC), get a copy. It can serve as the basis for your business FSIC ([RA 11032 Sec. 12](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11032_2018.html)).
- [ ] Check your city or municipality's own BOSS page for any extra requirements.
## Steps (worked example: Quezon City online)
1. Create an account on QC E-Services ([QC guide](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qcitizen-guides/opening-a-business-in-qc/)).
2. Go to **Business One-Stop Shop** and choose "Apply for Mayor's Permit (Business)".
3. Fill out the Unified Application Form.
4. Upload your ID, DTI/SEC/CDA registration, proof of location and photos.
5. Wait for your risk classification and assessment. QC classifies new permits as low risk (3 working days), medium (7) or high (20). Renewals take 3 days ([QC EO 38 s.2025](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qc-introduces-risk-based-system-for-faster-smarter-business-permit-processing/); [QC BPLD Citizen's Charter 2026](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/BPLD_2026-CC.pdf)).
6. Pay the tax bill online or at the treasury.
7. Download or claim your Mayor's Permit.
8. Prepare for inspections. In QC, these happen *after* the permit is issued, except the fire safety (FSIC) inspection ([QC EO 38 s.2025](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qc-introduces-risk-based-system-for-faster-smarter-business-permit-processing/)).
9. Register with BIR on or before you start operating. That means your first sale, or 30 days after your Mayor's Permit or DTI/SEC registration, whichever comes first ([RR 7-2024](https://bir-cdn.bir.gov.ph/BIR/pdf/RR%20No.%207-%202024.pdf)).
Other LGUs follow the same legal framework, but their portals and fees differ.
## Deadlines, renewals and penalties
- **Validity:** A business permit is valid for 1 year. Your LGU renews either in January or on the anniversary of issuance ([RA 11032 Sec. 11(e)](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11032_2018.html)).
- **Paying business tax:** Local business taxes are due within the first 20 days of January, or of each quarter if paid quarterly ([RA 7160 Sec. 167](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1991/ra_7160_1991.html)).
- **Late payment:** LGUs may charge a surcharge of up to 25%, plus interest of up to 2% a month for at most 36 months ([RA 7160 Sec. 168](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1991/ra_7160_1991.html)). The actual rates are in your local revenue code.
- **Fire safety certificate (renewals):** BFP must give your LGU the FSIC within 3 working days. If it does not, you are deemed to hold a temporary valid FSIC and your permit is renewed automatically. Tell BFP about any renovations 30 working days before the permit expires ([RA 11032 Sec. 12](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11032_2018.html)).
- **If the office doesn't act in time:** An application that is complete but not acted on within the processing time is deemed approved ([RA 11032, new Sec. 10 of RA 9485](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11032_2018.html)).
## Common problems and where to get help
- **Asked to submit the same document twice:** The business permit office may not require documents you already gave other LGU departments, such as a barangay clearance ([RA 11032, new Sec. 14 of RA 9485](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11032_2018.html)).
- **Fire extinguisher brand "required":** BFP staff may not sell or recommend specific brands ([RA 11032 Sec. 12(f)](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2018/ra_11032_2018.html)).
- **Quezon City BPLD:** [bpld@quezoncity.gov.ph](mailto:bpld@quezoncity.gov.ph), 8988-4242 loc. 8173 ([QC guide](https://quezoncity.gov.ph/qcitizen-guides/opening-a-business-in-qc/)).
- **Red tape complaints:** 8888 Citizens' Complaint Center; Contact Center ng Bayan 1-6565; ARTA at [complaints@arta.gov.ph](mailto:complaints@arta.gov.ph) ([LRA Citizen's Charter 2024, contact annex](https://lra.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/LRA_CC_03302024-1st-Edition.pdf)).
